What the app simulates

A short downtown ride

Explore the scene, request the concept vehicle, wait for a simulated pickup, enter the cabin, buckle up in the interface, ride, arrive, and exit. Cabin temperature, ambient sound, and pause controls affect the local presentation only.

The displayed pickup is Congress Avenue & 2nd Street; the destination is Congress Avenue & 7th Street. This short route follows Congress north through downtown and does not cross Lady Bird Lake. Fare-like, wait, and duration displays are illustrative, not current Tesla prices or service estimates.

Visual fidelity

Cinematic reconstruction

The city, Cybercab, and cabin are made from procedural geometry and original UI artwork. Lighting and post-processing aim for a cinematic look. The result is an artistic approximation, not a photogrammetric digital twin, a surveyed model, or an Unreal Engine benchmark.

  • Roads, building footprints, and water geometry are drawn from the bundled OSM extract.
  • Building heights use available map tags or estimates; facade details and many streetscape elements are invented or simplified.
  • The vehicle is an enclosed artistic pod (not manufacturer CAD) with a glass canopy and butterfly door. The phone and cabin screens are original layouts following Tesla's published rider steps, not pixel copies of Tesla software.
  • Frost Bank Tower, The Independent, and The Austonian use OSM footprints with added crowns so the skyline reads as Austin. The pink Capitol dome is placed at the real Congress terminus, just north of this extract.
  • No live traffic, real-time conditions, vehicle perception, dispatch, or driving policy is modeled. NPC cars, lights, and pedestrians are local immersion only.
Geographic data

OpenStreetMap extract

The bundled public/data/austin.json is derived from OpenStreetMap data for a compact downtown area around Congress Avenue, approximately 2nd through 7th Street. It includes nearby roads, building footprints, and Lady Bird Lake shoreline. It is a static snapshot, not a live tile or routing service, and it does not define Tesla's service boundary.

The extract's bounding box is west −97.754, south 30.252, east −97.738, north 30.272. Way geometry can extend beyond the box where a way crosses its edge. The lake shoreline relation is included to close the water outline; islands are omitted. The layer retains vehicle roads and pedestrian streets, while footways, paths, and cycleways are omitted. The checked-in snapshot contains 1,079 road ways, 1,011 closed building footprints, and 15 water polygons.

Height selection: use the OSM height tag when available, otherwise building:levels × 3.4 m, otherwise a 10 m default in the imported data. These fallbacks are estimates. The import script and additional provenance notes are in docs/RESEARCH.md.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0. The adapted map database remains under ODbL; retain its attribution and source metadata when redistributing it.

Source endpoints: Downtown bounding-box extract and Lady Bird Lake relation 32671.

Service references

Official Tesla information

Tesla's currently published pages describe Robotaxi service in limited areas of Austin. The actual app displays the service area and available vehicle assignment; no authoritative downloadable service-area polygon was found for this project. Third-party reporting on 31 August 2026 put the unsupervised Austin zone near ~288 square miles (north toward Pflugerville / US 183). This experience draws an approximate envelope on the phone map and keeps the downtown ride inside it — that ring is not official Tesla data.

Cybercab pickup uses the RGB front lightbar Tesla named the Megalamp (1 Oct 2026): the app shows a match color and the vehicle glows the same color; hazards flash while parked. The cabin flow follows the published rider guide: buckle, doors closed, Start Ride on the touchscreen or app.

Tesla operates both Cybercab and Model Y rides; the real vehicle depends on availability and group size. This concept always depicts a Cybercab. Product facts can change; consult Tesla's pages for current service details.

Platform

Runs in a WebGL 2 browser

This project is a Vite web app using Three.js. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux in a modern browser with WebGL 2 support; it does not package native executables. The bundled map works offline. DM Sans and Manrope are requested from Google Fonts, with generic sans-serif fallbacks if the network fonts are unavailable.

Local commands

npm install
npm run dev
